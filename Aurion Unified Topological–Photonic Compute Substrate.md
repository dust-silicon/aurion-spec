Abstract

This white paper presents the architecture, physical principles, fabrication strategy, and systems engineering roadmap for the Aurion Unified Topological–Photonic Compute Substrate (UTPCS) — a wafer-scale computational platform integrating classical, probabilistic, neuromorphic, tensor, photonic, and quantum computation within a single topological-photonic substrate.

Aurion departs fundamentally from conventional transistor-centric computing by introducing a multi-modal compute fabric built from Interfacial Skyrmion Cells (ISQ cells), photonic routing layers, magnonic coupling networks, sparse CMOS supervisory control, and topological coherence engineering. Unlike conventional CPUs, GPUs, TPUs, NPUs, and QPUs — each designed as separate specialized processors — Aurion dynamically reconfigures the same physical compute substrate into different computational modes depending on workload requirements.

The architecture is centered around a 4.7 inch × 4.7 inch wafer-scale silicon substrate, enabling unprecedented compute density, low-latency photonic interconnectivity, and large-scale distributed coherence domains. The system integrates:

* RISC-V RV64GCVQ control clusters
* Photonic tensor fabrics
* Magnonic wave processors
* Stochastic p-bit arrays
* Neuromorphic resonant networks
* Ambient-temperature interfacial skyrmion quantum cells
* Coherence-aware scheduling infrastructure

Aurion establishes a new category of computational infrastructure:

Computational Matter

where information processing emerges from topological states, resonant interactions, wave interference, and distributed physical dynamics rather than solely from Boolean transistor switching.
