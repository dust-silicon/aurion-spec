Abstract

This white paper introduces the Aurion Unified Topological–Photonic Compute Substrate (UTPCS), a wafer-scale computational architecture integrating deterministic classical computation, photonic tensor acceleration, stochastic probabilistic processing, neuromorphic resonant inference, and topological quantum computation into a unified physical substrate.

Aurion departs fundamentally from transistor-centric computing by treating computation as a distributed physical phenomenon emerging from:

* photonic propagation
* topological state encoding
* magnonic interactions
* resonant synchronization
* stochastic thermal dynamics
* coherent wave interference

The architecture is implemented on a 4.7 inch × 4.7 inch silicon wafer platform, enabling unprecedented compute density and interconnect bandwidth. Rather than relying on discrete specialized accelerators connected through electrical buses, Aurion physically partitions the wafer into dedicated computational domains:

* classical CPU domains
* tensor/GPU domains
* probabilistic p-bit regions
* neuromorphic resonant fabrics
* quantum coherence clusters

All domains communicate through a unified photonic mesh fabric and are orchestrated through coherence-aware global scheduling infrastructure.

Projected target capacities include:

* 64 classical superscalar RV64GCVQ cores
* 512 billion classical bits
* 50 billion stochastic GPU/p-bit elements
* 10–100 billion neuromorphic resonant units
* 1 billion independently addressable quantum clusters
* 64 qubits per quantum cluster
* theoretical aggregate capacity exceeding 64 billion qubits

Aurion establishes a new computational category:

Computational Matter

where information processing emerges directly from engineered physical dynamics distributed across a wafer-scale photonic-topological substrate.
